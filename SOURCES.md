# Sources and Editorial Policy

**Lifespan · Activity journal**
Catalog reviewed: October 23, 2026; individual source check dates are stored in `sources.json`.

## Editorial Scope & Purpose

This application is an activity journal for movement, learning, communication, and social connection across life stages. It is **not a developmental screening test, diagnostic tool, or clinical prescription**.

Browsing age ranges and session lengths are editorial suggestions to organize the catalog, not developmental deadlines or validated age cutoffs.

## Evidence Boundaries

1. **Developmental Monitoring vs. Screening**:
   Checklists and activity ideas support everyday monitoring and shared caregiver-child play. They do not replace standardized developmental screening by a qualified healthcare professional. AAP recommends formal screening at 9, 18, and 30 months, and autism screening at 18 and 24 months.

2. **Physical Activity & Movement**:
   Guidance draws from WHO physical activity and sedentary behaviour recommendations across the lifespan. Brief journal suggestions do not calculate or represent full whole-day activity recommendations.

3. **Responsive Caregiving & Social Connection**:
   Activities encourage voluntary, reciprocal interactions, consent, and autonomy. They do not score attachment security or prescribe relationship norms.

4. **Prematurity**:
   For infants born preterm, use adjusted (corrected) age for milestone comparisons through about two years with individual clinical guidance. This does not turn a browsing age into a universal feeding or exercise eligibility rule.

## Sequence review

Later-adulthood browsing spans ages 65-120, with editorial prerequisite depth used to order related activities. This avoids equating increasing age with increasing difficulty. [WHO later-adulthood activity guidance](https://www.who.int/tools/your-life-your-health/life-phase/later-adulthood/being-active-in-later-adulthood) supports adapting activity to physical condition; it does not validate these exact exercise thresholds or graph edges. Completion records are user confirmations of the displayed criteria.


## October 2026 remediation

The historical review report is absent from this checkout. Infant activities use current readiness and optional related ideas, with no mandatory developmental completion chain. Exact session durations and educational goals remain editorial. Whole-day or weekly activity recommendations are separate from journal session time.

New sources cover [feeding readiness](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/when-what-and-how-to-introduce-solid-foods.html), [choking prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html), [activity intensity](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html), [supported strength](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/), [sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/), [cognitive-health limits](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/brain-health/cognitive-health-and-older-adults), and [individual savings goals](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/). Source inclusion does not validate every editorial instruction.

## Developmental classification policy

Toddlerhood now browses 12–36 months and early childhood starts at three years. The catalog prioritizes observed abilities and functional readiness. Adult bands remain organizational conventions, and the existing adolescent catalog is narrower than WHO's 10–19 definition. Neither school entry nor adult social roles determine biological readiness. The historical developmental comparison report is absent from this checkout; its detailed review claims cannot be independently verified here. The source library includes the newly checked boundary references.
